Accessible course files · a do-it-yourself guide

Build your own accessibility prompt

A prompt that fixes course files safely has five parts. Walk through them below, make your choices, and copy the finished prompt into the AI tool your institution approves. You don't need us, or any prompt experience, to use it.

From the TRENDS 2026 session "PDFs, Scanned Manuals, Handouts—Oh My!" · Kelsey Dailey & Josh Bolakowski, Lake Michigan College

Before you start

  • Use an approved tool. Check which AI tools your institution allows for course materials. To get a fixed file back, the tool has to accept file uploads.
  • Course content only. Never upload anything with student names, grades or other student records.
  • Not yours? License first. For publisher chapters, vendor manuals and textbook scans, ask the publisher for an accessible copy before you rebuild anything.
  • Keep your original. Save a copy before you start, so nothing is ever lost.

Ask for the "needs your eyes" list

This is the most important line in the prompt. The AI handles the mechanical work, and you make the judgment calls. The list tells you exactly which calls are yours, so you aren't hunting for them.

List options

After you run it: first pass, not the verdict

  1. Work the list. Make each call on the "needs your eyes" list yourself.
  2. Verify the whole file. Check it against WCAG yourself, not only the flagged items. Run your app's accessibility checker, and use a screen reader if you can. A checker finds the big things but can't judge quality: junk alt text still passes.
  3. Turn every miss into a rule. If you catch something the AI missed, add a line to your guardrails so it's caught next time. Your prompt gets better each time you use it.

Last step

Make it yours. This is a starting point.

The prompt you built here is a first draft, not a finished tool. It works best as the start of a conversation.

  1. Feed it to your AI, then talk it through. Paste the prompt in, then tell the AI about your course, your students and the files you're working with. Ask what else it needs to know, and adjust the prompt together until it fits.
  2. Align it with your people and your policies. Check it against what your faculty and their courses actually need, your institution's AI and accessibility policies, and any guardrails your college already has in place.
  3. Always double-check what the AI gives you. Every file, every time. AI can be confidently wrong, and the final call belongs to you.
